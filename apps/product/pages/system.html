STAGE 01 · apps/api/src/producers

Start with the records nobody designed for you.

What enters. Socrata, ArcGIS, Carto, and CKAN endpoints — each city publishes on its own platform, with its own column names, watermark behavior, pagination style, and update rhythm.

What it produces. A consistent event envelope — {"source", "type", "city_id"} — that preserves which platform a record came from while making downstream stages city-agnostic.

Why it holds up.

  • City-specific producers keep source quirks intact instead of flattening them away.
  • Watermark columns differ per dataset; each registration declares its own, with id-key fallback chains when a column renames (job__ → job_number → id).
  • Text watermarks are normalized with sentinel exclusion, so placeholder rows never masquerade as fresh data.
  • Feeds that cannot stream (snapshot-mode endpoints, year-sliced publications) are ingested honestly as snapshots, not pretending to be realtime.
  • NYC-only context feeds ride the same registry, with their own platform and cadence: the Marshal’s executed evictions endpoint (6z8x-wfk4) polls on Socrata every 15 minutes, a validation signal that never feeds the LIMS score.

STAGE 02 · apps/api/src/schemas/models.py

Make different vocabularies comparable.

What enters. Raw records speaking city dialect: a Chicago permit and a Philly permit share a concept, not a schema.

What it produces. Typed events — permit, complaint, license, deed — with validated occurred_at, coordinates, and typed fields, enforced by pydantic models.

Why it holds up.

  • Field maps resolve city-specific columns into the shared contract.
  • Unknown fields are preserved for inspection, never dropped into a generic bucket.
  • Malformed rows fail loudly at the type boundary instead of poisoning aggregates downstream.

STAGE 03 · apps/api/src/spatial/h3_indexer.py

Give every event a neighborhood-sized address.

What enters. Typed events carrying WGS84 coordinates.

What it produces. H3 cells at resolutions 7, 8, and 9 — macro context, submarket, and neighborhood scale — plus division and submarket joins from the city registry.

Why it holds up.

  • The multi-resolution hierarchy lets the same event answer a metro question and a block question.
  • Division and submarket boundaries come from the registry, not ad-hoc polygons.
  • A flat record becomes a place with exactly one canonical address per resolution.

STAGE 04 · apps/api/src/features/lims_calculator.py

Let time and type change the meaning.

What enters. Spatially-addressed event streams, arriving at each feed’s real cadence.

What it produces. Time-decayed, interpretable features — CapEx density, permit velocity, 311 shift dynamics, license activity — composed into the Leading Indicator Momentum Score.

Why it holds up.

  • Every ingredient stays inspectable; the score can always be decomposed into its parts.
  • Decay windows respect feed cadence — a 3-minute feed and a snapshot feed are not treated as equals.
  • Feature definitions live in one auditable calculator, not scattered across consumers.

STAGE 05 · apps/api/src/export/snapshot_builder.py

Put the signal where a person can use it.

What enters. Features and model artifacts spanning 6-, 12-, and 18-month horizons.

What it produces. Versioned edge snapshots for fast reach, a FastAPI serving surface for queries, and the live dashboard view.

Why it holds up.

  • Snapshots are versioned — nothing at the edge silently overwrites its own provenance.
  • The serving layer exposes the same contracts the site documents; no parallel truth.
  • Model horizons answer three different questions (6m tactical, 12m swing, 18m structural) instead of one overfit number.
