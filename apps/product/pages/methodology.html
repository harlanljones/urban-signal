THE INGREDIENTS

Four inputs,
each with a job.

Every ingredient is a municipal signal family with a different lead time. The weights below are the calculator’s defaults (lims_calculator.py).

α · 0.35

CapEx density

Recorded construction value, time-decayed with a 180-day half-life. Money that owners commit today is the slowest, loudest signal on the board.

β · 0.25

Permit velocity

The acceleration of permit filings — not the count, the direction of its change. Filing surges precede visible construction by months.

γ · 0.20

311 shift dynamics

The changing mix of service requests. Complaint categories lead neighborhood transitions — renovation complaints arrive before scaffolding does.

δ · 0.20

License activity

Hospitality license activations from SLA feeds. New licenses trace where commercial foot traffic is expected, before leases are public.

THE SHAPE OF THE MATH

Comparable, decayed, decomposable.

The score is a weighted combination of standardized ingredients — the same formulation documented in the repository README and implemented in lims_calculator.py.

LIMSh = α·Z(CapExh) + β·Z(PermitVelocityh) + γ·Z(ΔR311,h) + δ·Z(LicSLA,h)

Z-scores, not raw counts

Each ingredient is standardized, so a busy metro and a quiet one are comparable — the score measures deviation from typical, not volume.

Decay is explicit

CapEx decays with a 180-day half-life (λ = ln 2 / 180, tunable via CAPEX_HALFLIFE_DAYS). Old money fades; the math says so out loud.

Thresholds are public

Catalyst alerts fire at LIMS ≥ 85.0 (LIMS_THRESHOLD). The same threshold the dashboard’s catalyst feed uses — no private dial.

THE HORIZONS

Three horizons,
three questions.

Model implementations cover 6-, 12-, and 18-month horizons with spatio-temporal leakage controls. Described at contract level; no performance claims are published.

6M

Tactical

Which cells are moving now — the dashboard’s heatmap and catalyst feed operate here.

12M

Swing

Where momentum is building toward a change of state within a market cycle.

18M

Structural

Which submarkets are compounding into durable regime shifts.

The model contract. Implementations live in apps/api/src/models: a spatio-temporal GNN (st_gnn.py), a cross-network macro model (dcn_v2.py), and a quantile LGBM (quantile_lgbm.py), with calibration.py, SHAP-based attribution (explainability.py — the dashboard’s waterfall), and leakage controls in validation.py. Retraining runs walk-forward cross-validation and exports ONNX artifacts (the shipped st_gnn_12m.onnx, dcn_v2_macro.onnx). Splits respect both space and time — models are evaluated on later periods and areas they did not train on.

THE BOUNDARY

What LIMS is not.

  • Not a price forecast. LIMS measures momentum in municipal activity. It does not predict transaction prices or returns.
  • Not a performance claim. No accuracy, precision, or ROI numbers are published anywhere on this site — by policy, not omission.
  • Not uniform across metros. Feed mix differs by city; a licenses-led metro and a permits-led metro are read differently. Coverage is stated per metro.
  • Not realtime. Signals move at feed cadence — minutes for some feeds, snapshots for others. Cadence is declared, never implied.
  • Not the number on the hero. The 68/100 panel above is an interface example with illustrative values. Only its weights are the calculator’s real defaults.
